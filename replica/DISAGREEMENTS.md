# Disagreements between the replica and the engine

Every disagreement above tolerance (1e-9 relative, with a floor of 1e-6 dollars, 1e-12 on rates) found while the replica was built and in its fix round, with its cause and what was done. The causes are: a replica bug or misreading (fixed); a convention no document stated (written down as a method note in `docs/oss/mid-size-bank.md`, the replica aligned); a document that contradicts the code (reported to the maintainer); an engine or kernel defect (reported; the engine one was fixed in the fix round with an approved engine change; a kernel bug would stop the build).

The first reading of each model was compared family by family with an engine run of the bank on the run-off basis. Fixed and floating loans, the swap, the undrawn lines, the balances held at book, all deposit families, the CD book, NII, EVE on both bases and both gap ladders tied on the first reading. On the coverage book (fix round) every family tied on the first reading except the CD book (D5).

## D1. FTP of the two checking families read another deposit history (engine defect; fixed in the fix round)

- **Seen.** In the FTP block the non-interest checking and interest checking rows differed from the replica by 4e-5 to 2e-4 relative on the five rates and on the 12-month accrual and margin; their monthly balances agreed to month 13, then differed by up to 1.4e-3 relative. The book's credits, mismatch, funding margin and NIM rate moved with them. Sized by the review: 0.03 bp on the two all-in rates, about $4,200 on 12-month credits and the mismatch.
- **Cause.** The FTP block built the deposits without the run's `nmdWarmup` (`packages/engine/src/ftp/computeFtp.ts`, its `makeInstrument` call), so they took the kernel's own warmup series, which ends in September 2025, six months before the as-of date; the history was threaded everywhere but there. The gap had been disclosed as a limitation, which is why the first round filed it as a stated convention; the review judged it an engine inconsistency.
- **Done.** The FTP block now passes the base scenario's warmup history into its instrument build. A run without `nmdWarmup` is byte-identical, and the reviewer's parity scripts, run before and after the change, are identical except their three warmup runs, whose only difference is the `ftp` block. The two family rows and five book cells are targets again and tie (book NIM rate by method note M6).

## D2. Mortgage seasonality: the documents say calendar, the kernel keys on loan age (doc-versus-code conflict in the kernel; method note M1)

- **Seen.** The first reading followed the documents and put the seasonal pattern on the calendar month. Month-1 principal was 15 percent off on the MBS pool and 24 percent off on the residential mortgages, in every scenario.
- **Cause.** Two documents call the pattern a calendar one: the parameter help text ("Amplitude of the calendar pattern", `packages/engine/src/contracts/paramSchema.ts:195` before this slice) and the kernel's mortgage header ("calendar seasonality", `packages/kernel/src/math/instruments/mortgage.ts:15`). The kernel's code keys it on the loan's age (`prepay.ts:33`: "seasoning/seasonality use age + m"). The CPR the engine implies, month by month, is the refinance S-curve times exactly 1 - 0.10 cos(2 pi (age - 1) / 12) on the pool (amplitude 10). On this bank every loan is a whole number of years old at 31 March, so the age-keyed pattern troughs in April and peaks in October, where a calendar pattern of the same shape would trough in January and peak in July. The first reading told the two apart by 15 to 24 percent.
- **Kind.** A conflict between the documents and the kernel's code, not a gap in the documents. The decision was to relabel now and leave the calendar for later: the kernel's age-keyed math stays; the help text now says "loan-age pattern"; the kernel header line is reworded in this copy's kernel comments, since the kernel is frozen; M1 says the pattern follows loan age; an opt-in calendar-keyed option is later engine work. No result changes.
- **Done.** The replica follows the code: 1.8e-14 relative afterwards. Sized by the review with the kernel's own shape keyed on the calendar month: base EVE -$0.17M, NII over 12 months -$0.10M, -$1.71M EVE at -200 bp.

## D3. Mortgage burnout accumulation (the replica misread the unit; method note M2)

- **Seen.** With the seasonality fixed, base and the up shocks tied, but at -100 and -200 bp, where the loans go in the money, the late-life balances were far off: up to 570 times on the last months of the residential pool.
- **Cause.** The first reading summed the incentive in percentage points over the months before the current one. The help text states the unit as "% per point" (`paramSchema.ts:196`): 0.05 is 0.05 percent of log-decay per percentage-point month, which is 0.05 per decimal month, so read literally it gives the convention the engine runs; the misreading was the replica's. The timing was stated nowhere: the burnout factor the engine implies, -ln(B) / burnoutDecay, equals the running sum of max(0, note rate - mortgage rate) in decimals with the current month included (0.00708678 in month 1 at -200 bp, against an incentive of 70.87 bp).
- **Kind.** A replica misreading of a stated unit, and a timing convention no document stated. M2 writes both down, including that the current month's incentive counts, a choice (standard burnout reads the exposure before the month); the replica aligned. Afterwards: 2.2e-14 relative at -200 bp.

## D4. Interest checking's beta: the data note said 0.25, the book carried a static-rate deposit (the bank file's type; fixed)

- **Seen.** The data note said interest checking "pays 1.00 percent with a fixed beta of 0.25". The engine paid a flat 1 percent in every scenario and banded the family as pure runoff in the repricing gap; the replica tied on that basis (DEFINITIONS D10).
- **Cause.** The bank file typed interest checking as `nmd`, the engine's non-interest-bearing deposit (maps `nmd` to DDA), which pays its static `depositRate`. Its `beta` field does not move the paid rate, and the engine's repricing gap keeps that type as pure runoff, which the engine states (`packages/engine/src/gap/gapEngine.ts:39`). The kernel's comment on the field, carried into the generated intake table (`docs/import-formats.md:164`), says the beta drives a repricing-gap split; that is a second doc-versus-code conflict, of the H1 kind, and goes to the maintainer with it. The engine does what its own documents say, so this is the bank's binding, not an engine defect.
- **Done.** The bank's interest checking is an `nmd-b` with a beta S-curve from 0.24 to 0.26 around 3.5 percent (adjustment 0.5 a month), so it reprices with a beta of about 0.25 and pays about 0.93 percent at the as-of date; the assumption set binds the same curve. Its decay parameters are unchanged; as a beta deposit its rate response reads the market rate less the paid rate. The bank's figures move: NII over 12 months (the analysis, with new volume) $177.2M to $177.8M, 3.54 to 3.56 percent of assets; run-off EVE $1,323.5M to $1,318.9M at base and $1,143.3M to $1,124.6M at +200 bp, a change of -14.7 percent against -13.6. A wider beta curve (0.15 to 0.35) gave -15.4 percent at +200 bp, outside the maintainer's 10 to 15 percent realism band the bank test holds; the curve nearest the stated "fixed beta of 0.25" stays inside it.

## D5. A CD cohort below the minimum live balance leaves the book without a cash flow (convention not stated; method note M7)

- **Seen.** On the coverage book, month-1 principal of the CD book was $499.68 higher in the replica than in the engine, in every scenario; every later month tied, and the engine's principal over the life sums to $45,000,000.32 against a notional of $45,000,500.
- **Cause.** The replica's first reading paid out a cohort that fell below the $1,000 minimum live balance as principal. The engine drops it from the book with no cash flow; its month-1 coupon and early withdrawal are still paid. The kernel documents the minimum live balance (`cdBook.ts:52`), not what happens to the balance below it.
- **Kind.** A convention no document stated, written down as M7; the replica aligned (4.9e-13 afterwards). It is also a modelling point for the maintainer: the balance is not repaid, so principal is not conserved, by up to the minimum on each cohort that reaches it. Quillfield's cohorts never reach it.
- **an earlier build step.** The corrected-v1 convention lowers the minimum to $1 of walk balance (DEFINITIONS row K3, a stated input). On it the coverage book's $500 cohort runs to maturity and the CD book's principal sums to its notional, $45,000,500, in the replica and the engine alike; the original convention keeps the $1,000 minimum and the shortfall.

## D6. SEG replacement vintages of a deposit read the kernel's own warmup series (closed in an earlier build step on the corrected convention)

- **Seen.** On the SEG demo, interest checking's outstanding SEG differs from month 13 on, by up to $49,439 at month 120, and its up-shock constant-balance NII by up to $2.64 a month. Its base and down-shock NII, its EBP and every other family tie, at most $6.6e-5 apart. The book series and the bank print carry the same difference.
- **Cause.** The kernel's replacement vintage of a deposit (`makeVintage`) is built without the parent's warmup, so it reads the kernel's own hand-typed series, 297 months to September 2025, while the parent reads the run's SOFR history. A black-box check of the vintage instrument showed this: its history is not its parent's. the limitations list states it for the flat warmup ("the replacement vintages keep the kernel series"), and the history kind inherits it. The difference reaches only interest checking: it is the bank's one beta deposit with the regime amplifier on (alpha 1.0), and the amplifier fires on the up-shocked vintages, on a moving-average window that mixes the kernel's 2025 values with the path. Non-interest checking also runs alpha 1.0, but its amplifier stays dormant and it ties.
- **Kind.** A kernel behavior the documents state, which the replica cannot follow without copying a kernel constant. It was queued for a kernel review; the kernel is not changed.
- **Done in an earlier build step and its fix round (original convention, now history).** The replica builds every deposit vintage on the run's history (method note M10 records the engine's way). The exclusion is cut to the cause. A vintage reads the warmup only through its regime amplifier, while its moving-average window still holds warmup months, and only where the rate is above that average. The replica finds the first such month itself (DEFINITIONS row S13) and writes it in the SEG targets as `vintageWarmupOnset`. For interest checking that is SEG index 13 (the 25 bp walks) and up-shock NII month 3 (the 100 bp walk); base and down NII never read it. The check compares every cell of the family before that point: SEG indices 0 to 12, up-shock NII months 1 and 2, all base and down NII. From that point it cuts the family's series and reads the book's series and the bank print less that family's (for the bank print, the family's outstanding SEG less its opening, the bank print of a deposit to $3e-8). The family's up-shock 12-month total is left out, since it sums months past the cut, and the book's is read less it. The family's peak is compared when the engine's peak month falls before the cut (it does: month 1). The book's own peak is compared raw; it ties. The derivation is the replica's: if the engine's vintages read their warmup earlier than the replica's do, the earlier cells differ and the check fails, so the cut cannot hide a difference before it. Every other SEG cell is compared.
- **Closed.** On the corrected-v1 convention the engine rebuilds a plain deposit's replacement vintage on the run's history, unshifted (DEFINITIONS row K4), which is what the replica always built. The SEG demo runs corrected-v1, so the onset cut, `vintageWarmupOnset` and row S13 are gone and the accept check compares the whole SEG block, interest checking included: 9,808 SEG cells and 780 NII cells, largest SEG gap $6.7e-5 (the D7 floor is $2.4e-3), NII gaps at most $1.6e-7. The replica's interest-checking series did not change; the engine's moved onto them. On the original convention the vintages still read the kernel's series, and the replica refuses a SEG block on that convention rather than model it.

## D7. SEG precision and the peak of a near-zero series (comparison conventions, not model disagreements)

- **Seen.** At the 1e-6 dollar floor, a few hundred SEG cells failed by about $1e-5 although every input tied, and four families' peak month disagreed (cash, C&I floating, home equity lines, the floating advance).
- **Cause.** SEG divides an interest difference by the shock: x 12 / (2 x 25 bp), 2,400 times, so the $1e-9 rounding of interest becomes about $2e-6 of SEG. A one-month position or a monthly floater reprices at once, so its outstanding SEG is 0 plus rounding after month 1, and its peak is an argmax over rounding noise.
- **Done.** SEG cells read a floor of $2.4e-3: the $1e-6 floor carried through the same divisor, a $1e-6 interest difference. The largest SEG gap left is $6.6e-5. A peak whose value is within that floor has its month left out; its value is still compared.

## D8. The commitment dials read the undrawn lines' $1,000 million as the commitment face (closed 2026-11-05)

- **Seen.** Every cell of the survival-path demo tied on the first reading. The dials' undrawn was $850 million and their LCR reference $85 million, while the bank file, the data note and the ladder called the line's $1,000 million undrawn.
- **Cause.** The seeded setup set the dials' face to the undrawn lines' $1,000 million. The dials' definition takes the face as the commitment extended, with Ubar of it drawn.
- **Kind.** A reading of the bank's values, not a disagreement in value. The replica followed the dials' definition as written and was not bent.
- **Closed.** The decision of 2026-11-05: "the $1,000M is undrawn". The ladder's undrawn face is the amount undrawn now, U. The dials derive the face U / (1 - Ubar) and read the LCR reference on U. The severe row draws the requirement over 12 months (DEFINITIONS L10, L12). The engine and the replica were both changed to that rule, the replica from the decision's words. The demo now reads:
  - face $1,176.47 million;
  - requirement and severe draw $235.29 million;
  - LCR reference $100 million;
  - survival 203 days without the borrowing line, 322 with it.
  Every cell tied on the first reading.

## Conventions written down without a disagreement

The replica's first reading of four more conventions tied at once, but no document stated them, so each became a method note: M3 (the single monthly mortality and the balance it applies to), M4 (a deposit month's path step, closure age, moving-average window and final payout), M5 (the CD walk's remaining months and maturity month), M6 (the FTP book NIM rate).

In an earlier build step the demos added two more. Both were read off the engine before the replica was written, so neither could show as a disagreement, and on both the replica checks consistency with the engine, not correctness:
- M8: the regulatory convention's four parameters (pass-through cap 0.5, core cap 0.9, a 5-year life, ten rungs). They are inputs to a convention, not outputs: the engine's defaults, inferred from the children the engine wrote and confirmed by printing the engine's defaults constant. `replica/inputs/regulatory-convention-v1.json` restates them as stated inputs that both sides read. The replica checks that the engine slots, prices and ladders the deposits by them; a wrong parameter would pass.
- M9: the SEG replacement-vintage rule, a convention read from engine behavior (vintage instruments the kernel's own `makeVintage` returned for each instrument type); the replica checks consistency with it, not its correctness. Every constant-balance SEG and NII cell of the SEG demo depends on it.

The six standardized shocks are not in this list. Until an earlier build step fix round the replica read the shock vectors from the setup, which the engine had computed, so they too were checked for consistency only. Now the replica computes them from the published formula (method note M11, `alm_replica/srp.py`), runs its own, and holds the setup's to them at 1e-12 bp (largest gap 4.3e-14 bp). The long-rate magnitude is the 225 bp of the July 2024 recalibration, as M11 states it; typing 150 bp into the replica instead fails the setup's vectors by up to 67 bp, so the check now catches a wrong shock parameter.

Every other demo measure tied on the first reading: the spread-consistent basis, the per-position results, the fixed-rate ladder, constant prepayment and the stress ladder.

The liquidity work added the term-deposit role and its renewal share, the buffer from the HQLA tags, the dials-linked severe draw, the commitment dials and the survival path with the borrowing line (DEFINITIONS L7 to L15). Every cell tied on the first reading; D8 recorded one reading of the bank's values, which the decision of 2026-11-05 closed. In the fix round the CD renewal share rolls the maturities only (L7), and three edited variants (a path that never goes negative, mixed renewal rows, an untagged buffer position) tied against the engine cell for cell.

The deposit-forms work added Quillfield's funding tenor (DEFINITIONS F13) and, on the deposit desk, the straight-line runoff, component decay with its full-runoff life, the full beta form under frozen and shocked-level slotting with the other deposits on the regulatory split (DF9), and the replicating-portfolio credit on the base scenario and at every scenario (DF1 to DF16). Every measure tied on the first reading, every cell, so no new disagreement is logged (no D8). The mixed-book rule read clearly from its definition, and the replica built it before any desk cell was compared; on the desk the frozen and shocked-level runs differ in the money market book only, as the rule intends. Each of the fourteen provisional pins equals the replica's value to at most 6.5e-16 relative.

The corrections added the corrected-v1 convention: the CD walk on the path's curve plus the static TLP (DEFINITIONS row K2), the $1 CD minimum (K3), and SEG deposit vintages on the run's history (K4). The replica built K2 and K3 from their written definitions, and K4 was already its own reading. Quillfield, the coverage book and all seven demos tied on the first reading, every cell, with no new disagreement. The CD book now moves with every scenario: on Quillfield the +200 bp first-year CD runoff is $781.5M against $739.4M at base, where the original convention gives $739.3M in both.

Closed (P1): the per-position results of a balance held at book. Before the change both sides read pv 0 for the three non-rate-sensitive positions in every scenario, because the pass discounted cash flows the positions do not have, while EVE counts the family at par ($250M). The tie-out certified the 0 the engine emitted. Now the server values such a position at par, and the replica builds the same rule from DEFINITIONS row I7: pv is the balance at the cut-off in every scenario, the value its EVE gives the family (E4). The 15 cells (3 positions by 5 scenarios) tie, and every family's rows sum to its EVE.

## Within tolerance, for the record

The largest differences left are summation order: 1.2e-12 relative on a monthly repricing gap cell, 2.1e-13 on a position's monthly principal, 2.3e-13 on the coverage book's all-in EVE, at most 8.6e-6 dollars absolute anywhere outside SEG (SEG: D7). No kernel bug was found; the one engine defect (D1) was fixed. D6 is a stated kernel behavior on the review queue.
