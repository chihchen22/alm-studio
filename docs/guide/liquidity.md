# Liquidity: the stress ladder and the survival path

A liquidity stress asks how long the bank's liquid assets last if depositors leave, wholesale funding is not renewed and borrowers draw their lines, all at once. This page covers the stress ladder, the buffer, the survival path, the commitment dials, and the Quillfield figures behind Step 8 and one of the More demos.

## The idea and why a bank cares

A rate shock works through earnings and value over months and years, and a bank can carry a large value loss for a long time; a liquidity shock works in days, and a bank fails the week it cannot meet withdrawals. The questions a treasurer needs answered are plain: how much can be raised from the securities on hand, how fast could the deposits leave, what do the undrawn lines draw in a stress, and how many days does the buffer cover before the bank must borrow. The regulatory liquidity coverage ratio answers a 30-day version of that question with prescribed factors; the survival horizon on this page is the internal version, run on the bank's own projected cash flows under outflow rates the bank chooses, and read out to the month the buffer runs dry.

## The method as Studio computes it

The stress ladder runs 12 months on the base rate path. Every position carries a liquidity role: the cash and securities are the buffer stock, the loans produce inflows, the two checking families form a transactional pool, savings and money market a rate-sensitive pool, the advances are wholesale funding and the certificates of deposit are term deposits. Each row of the ladder (business as usual, moderate stress, severe stress) states its rates, and in each month:

$$\text{net}_m = \text{inflow}_m - \text{pool}_{m-1}\,\rho - \text{maturing}_m\,(1 - \text{renewal}) - \text{CD}_m - \frac{U\,\delta}{12}, \qquad \text{cum}_m = \sum_{j\le m} \text{net}_j,$$

where each deposit pool falls by its own outflow rate $\rho$ each month, wholesale maturities leave less the renewal share, the certificates of deposit leave as they mature less their renewal share plus every early withdrawal in full, and the undrawn lines draw $\delta$ of the undrawn amount $U$ over the first twelve months and nothing after. The inflows are the loans' principal on the base path, read at a slower speed on the stress rows for loans that carry a constant prepayment. Survival is the first month in which the buffer plus the cumulative net goes below zero, interpolated from the month before, in months and in days (months times 365 over 12).

The buffer is read from the positions when the setup states none: each buffer asset at its face less the haircut of its liquidity level (none for Level 1, 15 percent for Level 2A, 50 percent for Level 2B, the public LCR haircuts), summed with the derivation shown.

The survival path carries the severe row's recursion past month 12 on the run's own balances, to the horizon the setup asks for, and reports the crossing, the peak shortfall and its month, and the term-funding need (the peak less the buffer). A borrowing line the bank has received is read as a second buffer, capacity less its haircut, in the path only; it never enters the ladder's buffer.

The commitment dials size the draw. From the amount undrawn now $U$, an expected utilization $\bar U$ and a tail utilization $U_{99}$: the commitment face is $U/(1-\bar U)$, the buffer requirement is $(U_{99} - \bar U)$ of the face, and the severe row draws that requirement evenly over twelve months, a rate of $(U_{99} - \bar U)/(1 - \bar U)$ on $U$. The dials also give a contingent liquidity charge in basis points on the undrawn amount and an LCR reference, the regulatory factor times $U$, shown beside the path for comparison.

## Conventions and their choices

**Principal only.** No interest flow enters the ladder or the path.

**Faces, not market values.** The buffer counts each security at its face. A real sale would realize the market price; a stress in which rates have risen would realize less than face.

**No Level 2 caps.** The regulatory caps on Level 2 assets (40 percent of the stock, 15 percent for Level 2B) are not applied. On Quillfield the Level 2A share sits a little over that cap.

**Early withdrawals are the base path's.** The certificates of deposit are broken as the base scenario projects; a stress does not raise them.

**The LCR reference is not an LCR.** It is the dials' commitment outflow only, the factor times the undrawn amount. The survival path is not a regulatory metric.

**Illustrative rates.** The stress rates are the round starting values of the data note, not fitted. The business-as-usual row leaks 0.4 percent of the savings pool a month and 0.1 percent of the checking pool; the severe row takes 8 percent and 1.5 percent, renews 20 percent of maturing wholesale funding and 30 percent of maturing certificates of deposit, and draws the dials' requirement on the lines. I chose round values so that a reader can see each one on the setup and change it.

## Start here on this page

**Step 8, Survival path and dials (liquidity).** Open shows Liquidity with the Survival path section. On Run setups change a severe stress rate, press Save as a new version, launch it, and read how many days the buffer lasts. Lowering the savings outflow from 8 to 4 percent a month adds months; raising the CD renewal share does the same. On the page itself the borrowing line can be switched off, which takes the reading back to the 203 days above, and the dials can be edited, with the severe draw following them.

**More demos, Constant prepayment and the stress inflow line.** On Run setups pick the illustrative assumption set, which has no constant prepayment, press Save as a new version, launch it, and watch first-year inflows fall: the consumer and multifamily loans then return only their scheduled principal.
