# Funds transfer pricing (FTP)

Funds transfer pricing charges every asset for the funding it uses and credits every liability for the funding it provides, at a rate matched to its own cash flows. What is left on each side is margin; what is left in the middle is the bank's rate and liquidity mismatch. This page covers the par-matched rate, the floater's funding tenor, the replicating-portfolio credit on a deposit, and the Quillfield figures behind Steps 5 and 7.

## The idea and why a bank cares

Without transfer pricing, a lending unit that books a five-year fixed loan at 6 percent looks brilliant when short rates are at 2 percent and foolish when they are at 6, although nothing about the loan changed. FTP gives the loan a matched funding rate on the day it is booked, so the lender keeps the spread over that rate and the treasury keeps the bet on rates. The same for a deposit: a checking balance that pays nothing is worth the matched rate the bank would otherwise pay in the market, and the deposit unit is credited that rate. The bank's net interest income is then the sum of the lending margin, the funding margin and the mismatch center, and the mismatch is the only part the treasury is answerable for. For deposits, which have no contractual maturity, the matched rate is a judgment about how long the money stays, which is why the deposit credit has a method of its own below.

## The method as Studio computes it

Each position's transfer rate is the par-matched rate of its own principal schedule: the single rate that makes the present value of its principal and interest, discounted on the funding curve, equal to its notional. With discount factors $D(t_i)$ at the schedule's monthly dates, principal $P_i$ and start-of-month balance $B_{i-1}$:

$$r = \frac{N - \sum_i D(t_i)\,P_i}{\sum_i D(t_i)\,B_{i-1}\,\Delta t}, \qquad \Delta t = \tfrac{1}{12}.$$

Read against the risk-free curve the rate is the IR FTP; read against the risk-free curve plus the term liquidity premium it is the all-in FTP; the difference is the liquidity FTP. A rate-locked position carries its all-in rate flat; a floater refixes at each reset to the forward over its reset window plus the premium at its reset tenor; an interest-bearing deposit blends its beta share at the one-month forward with the rest at the all-in rate. The margin is the coupon less the all-in rate for an asset and the all-in rate less the coupon for a liability. The book adds up the assets' charges and the liabilities' credits, and the bridge reconciles lending margin plus funding margin plus mismatch, plus the swap's signed net settlement, to the base scenario's net interest income.

A floating loan that carries a funding tenor is priced in two legs: the rate leg at the one-month reset (the forward over the reset window on the curve's quoting basis) and the liquidity leg at the funding tenor (the term premium there). Without a tenor both legs are read as one term rate over the whole principal stream.

The replicating-portfolio credit prices a deposit as the funding it replaces. A portfolio of fixed-rate bullets on a pillar grid (1, 12, 36, 60 and 120 months) is built to match the deposit's life: the beta share rolls overnight on the one-month bullet, and the rest sits on the pillar whose life equals the deposit's or the two pillars that bracket it, weighted so the portfolio's life is the deposit's. The rate credit is the weighted par rate of that portfolio; the liquidity credit is the premium at the deposit's life on the whole balance; the total is their sum. The life comes from the deposit's own runoff: a straight-line runoff of $n$ months has a life of $(n+1)/2$ months, a decaying balance has the life of its full projected runoff, and a flat balance takes a stated cap.

## Conventions and their choices

**Base scenario only.** FTP is computed on the base scenario. A shocked FTP series is refused rather than published, because the shocked earnings convention (the shocked curve from the first step) is one coupon month off the convention FTP would need.

**Family grain.** Rates and margins are notional-weighted by family; positions are priced one by one and the per-position rows are not stored.

**Beta at the base rate.** An interest-bearing deposit's blend uses the beta at the base rate for the whole series; beta drift along the path is not in the FTP block.

**The credit's constructions.** Life-target is the default and the one the demos run. A strip construction uses no pillars: it strikes the non-repricing balance once at the par-matched rate of the deposit's own runoff schedule. A pillar construction puts the whole non-repricing balance on one named pillar. All three read the same curve and the same liquidity leg; they differ in the rate leg only. The credit may be read at every scenario, each on its own shifted curve with the premium held static, or held at the base curve.

**What a credit run leaves out.** A run that binds a credit prices transfer rates and carries no earnings or value scenario; those are read on the plain run of the same book.

## Start here on this page

**Step 5, Floater funding tenor (FTP).** Open shows FTP by family. Compare the floating rows: the rate leg is the same across them, and the home equity lines' 60-month funding tenor gives a higher liquidity leg than the construction loans' 24. There is no learner control for a tenor; it is a term on the position, set when the book is imported.

**Step 7, Replicating-portfolio credit (deposit FTP).** Open shows FTP with the Deposit credits card on the desk. Open the assumption set, in the dd-operating card set Construction to Strip on the runoff, press Save, then Use this copy and Launch. The strip prices the non-repricing balance at one rate, the par rate of its own 48-month straight-line runoff, instead of the 36- and 60-month blend; on this curve that rate is a few basis points lower, the liquidity credit does not move, and the card shows the figure.

**More demos, The credit at every scenario.** Open the assumption set, in the dd-savings card set Shocked scenarios to Hold base, press Save, then Use this copy and Launch. Holding the base curve keeps the +100 bp credit close to its base reading, since only the life moves; the card shows the figure.
