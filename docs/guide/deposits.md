# Deposits: decay, beta, and the conventions that slot them

Non-maturity deposits have no contractual date, so every measure on this site rests on an assumption about how long they stay and how fast their paid rate follows the market. This page covers the models Studio runs, the conventions that turn them into dated cash flows, the deposit desk that isolates each form, and the figures behind Step 6 and four of the More demos.

## The idea and why a bank cares

A checking balance can leave tomorrow, and most of it will still be there in five years. A money market account pays a rate the bank sets, which follows the market partly and late. Those two facts, balance persistence and partial repricing, are what make deposits worth so much to a bank and so hard to model: a deposit treated as overnight money makes every bank look liability-sensitive and worth little; a deposit treated as a five-year bond makes the same bank look hedged and worth a great deal. Value, gap and transfer pricing all move with that choice. The honest position is to run more than one convention and read the gap between them as the size of the judgment, which is what the demos on this page are for.

## The models as Studio computes them

**Decay.** A deposit balance is split into account-size buckets, and each bucket loses a share of its balance every month: a closure rate that ramps from an initial to a steady level, plus a rate response that rises along a logistic curve of the market rate (for a non-interest deposit) or of the market rate less the paid rate (for an interest-bearing one), scaled by a balance-size sensitivity, a burnout factor and a regime amplifier that fires when the rate runs above its own moving average. The moving average reads the shipped SOFR history up to the as-of month and the scenario's path after it. The last month pays out what is left.

**Beta.** An interest-bearing deposit's paid rate follows a target that is a share of the market rate, with the share itself a logistic S-curve of the rate level, and closes part of the gap to that target each month:

$$\beta(r) = \beta_{\min} + \frac{\beta_{\max} - \beta_{\min}}{1 + e^{-k\,(r - m)}}, \qquad D_t = D_{t-1} + \lambda\,\big(\beta(r_t)\,r_t - D_{t-1}\big).$$

The S-curve is built for a low beta at low rates and a higher one at high rates, since a bank passes little through when rates are near zero and more as depositors have somewhere else to go.

**The full beta form.** The money market demos run a fuller form: an intercept, the S-curve beta, and a damper that is stronger after a rise than after a fall, with the paid rate closing 60 percent of the gap to its target each month and balances held flat. It is a repricing model only; the balance does not decay.

**Straight-line runoff.** The simplest form: a balance that falls to zero in equal monthly steps over a stated horizon, with a life of $(n+1)/2$ months. It is the benchmark against which the behavioral forms are read.

**Component decay on a dated rate path.** A savings balance projected month by month on rates as they happened: each month a retention share stays, the survivors grow by a drift, and each percentage point the realized rate sits above its own trailing twelve-month average takes a further share off. A shocked scenario adds its shift to the window months only, so the benchmark stays on the realized path. The life is read on the full runoff, past the grid.

**Certificates of deposit.** A cohort book with an early-withdrawal hazard that rises with the gap between the offered rate and the contract rate; on the corrected convention the offers read the scenario's curve, so a rise in rates breaks more CDs early.

## Conventions and their choices

**Behavioral.** Each deposit runs on its own decay and beta model, and the gap ladders, value and FTP read the cash flows that result. This is the default on this site.

**Regulatory.** The standardized convention of the international standard, shown as a reference; the US has not adopted it. Each deposit is split into a stable core and a non-core slice. The pass-through is capped at 0.5, the core share at 0.9, and the core is laid out as ten equal rungs with a five-year average life (maturing at months 6, 18, and so on to 114), each a bullet at today's paid rate. The non-core slice leaves in one month: a one-month floater for an interest-bearing deposit, a one-month bullet at the paid rate otherwise. The engine applies one cap and one life to every deposit, where the standard sets different caps and lives for retail transactional, retail non-transactional and wholesale deposits.

**Shocked-level slotting.** The regulatory layout with a behavioral arm: a deposit carrying the full beta form reads its core share at each scenario's own rate, one less the damped beta there, over a stated core life (five years unless set), with no cap. A deposit without the form takes the regulatory split as above. On the desk the two full-beta demos differ in the money market book only.

**Frozen slotting.** The same layout with the core share held at the base date: one less the damped base beta, capped at 0.9, over five years, the same split in every scenario.

I chose to ship the regulatory and slotted conventions beside the behavioral one rather than in its place, so that a learner can run the same book on each and read the difference. Note that none of the conventions is fitted to data on this site; every parameter is a round illustrative value stated in [`DATA.md`](https://github.com/chihchen22/alm-studio/blob/main/DATA.md) and the two data notes ([`docs/oss/mid-size-bank.md`](https://github.com/chihchen22/alm-studio/blob/main/docs/oss/mid-size-bank.md), [`docs/oss/deposit-desk.md`](https://github.com/chihchen22/alm-studio/blob/main/docs/oss/deposit-desk.md)).

## Start here on this page

**Step 6, Full beta, frozen slotting (deposits).** Open shows Value (EVE) on the desk. On Run setups set the NMD convention to shocked-level, press Save as a new version, launch it, and compare the +100 bp value change on the money market row: the $10.1 million above becomes the $13.2 million.

**More demos, The regulatory deposit convention.** On Run setups set the NMD convention to behavioral, press Save as a new version, launch it, and watch base value rise by the $205 million above.

**More demos, A straight-line deposit runoff.** On Run setups set the NMD convention to regulatory, press Save as a new version, launch it, and watch the desk's value rise: the straight line becomes a capped core ladder with a five-year life, and a longer liability is worth less to the depositor and so costs the bank less. The screen shows the figure.

**More demos, Component decay on a dated rate path.** Open the assumption set, set Monthly retention to 0.99, press Save, then Use this copy and Launch, and read the savings life: with one percent leaving a month instead of two, the life more than doubles. The card shows the figure.

**More demos, The full beta form, shocked-level slotting.** Open the assumption set, set Core life to 7 years, press Save, then Use this copy and Launch, and read the +100 bp change on the money market row: a longer core is worth less as a liability under the rise, so the row's value falls by more and the desk gains more. The card shows the figure.

## What this page does not cover

The deposit credit in FTP, which turns a life into a transfer rate, is on [`ftp.md`](https://github.com/chihchen22/alm-studio/blob/main/docs/guide/ftp.md). The deposit pools in a liquidity stress, which read outflow rates rather than decay models, are on [`liquidity.md`](https://github.com/chihchen22/alm-studio/blob/main/docs/guide/liquidity.md). Earnings and value stand aside on the component decay and credit runs (the forms price volume or transfer rates, not a paid rate on a curve path), FTP stands aside on the component decay run too, and FTP stands aside on a full beta run; each run says so on its screen. Behavior on new deposit volume, beta drift in the gap ladders and the deposit history's basis step at the splice are in [`limitations.md`](https://github.com/chihchen22/alm-studio/blob/main/docs/guide/limitations.md).
